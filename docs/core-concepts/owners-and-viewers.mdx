Every slate, account and shared secret belongs to an `Owner`. The people who open a hosted slate are its viewers.

## Owner

An `Owner` is the identity that owns slates and resources. It has one of two types:

- `personal`: a single user.
- `organization`: an organization in ContextLayer. Every member of the organization can act for it.

A slate has exactly one Owner, chosen when the slate is created: you, or an organization you belong to. The CLI's `init` command selects the organization; see `npx @usecontextlayer/cli init --help`. The dashboard lists slates under Personal and under each of your organizations.

Accounts and shared secrets belong to an Owner independently of any slate. A slate refers to them and never copies them, so one Owner's accounts and secrets can serve several slates. See [Accounts](/core-concepts/accounts) and [Secrets](/core-concepts/resources/secrets).

A personal Owner is managed by that user, and an organization Owner by the organization's members. Managing an Owner covers its slates, its accounts and its secrets. Usage of a hosted slate is billed to the slate's Owner.

## Owner mode and viewer mode

Each declared resource has a mode that selects whose resource the hosted slate uses:

- `owner` selects the slate's Owner. Viewers use these resources through the slate without being able to manage the Owner's accounts or secrets.
- `viewer` selects the personal Owner of the person a request or job acts for. Each person uses their own account, their own secret value and their own database.

```json public/slate.json
{
  "connections": {
    "work-email": {
      "toolkit": "gmail",
      "mode": "viewer"
    }
  },
  "databases": {
    "notes": {
      "mode": "owner"
    }
  }
}
```

`notes` is in owner mode, so the slate's Owner supplies one database that every viewer shares. `work-email` is in viewer mode, so each viewer assigns one of their own Gmail accounts to it.

Viewer-mode resources work only on private slates. Locally, connections always use the developer's personal accounts, whatever the mode. [Resources](/core-concepts/resources) covers declarations.

## Viewers

A viewer is anyone who opens a hosted slate, in a browser or through an MCP client. Who can view depends on the slate's visibility:

| Slate | Who can view |
| --- | --- |
| Public | Anybody |
| Private, personal Owner | The owning user |
| Private, organization Owner | Members of the owning organization |

The organization rule always uses the slate's owning organization, regardless of which organization you have selected elsewhere. Viewing never grants management.

## Who can do what

| Action | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Open the app and use its MCP tools | Viewers |
| Start agent jobs and read their results | Viewers of a private slate |
| Assign an account to a viewer-mode connection, or save a viewer-mode secret | Viewers, each for themselves |
| Assign an account to an owner-mode connection | The owning user, or members of the owning organization |
| Clone, pull and push the source; see the slate in the dashboard; read agent instructions, transcripts and schedules; abort or delete jobs | The owning user, or members of the owning organization, whatever the visibility |
| Manage an Owner's accounts and secrets | That user, or members of that organization |

## Slate members

A slate member is a person participating in the slate. A viewer of a private slate becomes a member automatically once the slate's whole setup is complete for them: every connection declared by the app and its agents has an assigned account, their own for viewer-mode connections, and every viewer-mode secret has their value. Membership is checked when they save an assignment or a secret, in the chrome or through the API, and when they first use a viewer-mode resource.

Once someone is a member, ContextLayer migrates their viewer-mode databases and then installs their schedules for member-scoped [agents](/core-concepts/slate/agents#schedules), in the background. Membership stands while that work runs, and if it fails.

- Completed setup is the only way in; there is no explicit join.
- Creating a slate does not make its creator a member.
- Membership grants neither viewing nor management. It survives setup that later becomes incomplete.
- Public slates never have members.

Slate membership is separate from organization membership. A member of an organization can view its private slates, and becomes a member of a slate only after completing that slate's setup.

## Viewer sign-in

Hosted slates run on `contextlayer.xyz`, a domain separate from `usecontextlayer.com`, where you sign in to ContextLayer itself. When a private slate needs you to sign in, it sends you to ContextLayer's viewer sign-in, which signs you in with your ContextLayer account and returns you to the slate. Viewer sign-in identifies you as the same user, but it only lets you view the slate and use the capabilities ContextLayer delegates to the slate for you; it cannot manage anything.

The slate's own code never receives your credentials or your profile. ContextLayer verifies who you are and gives the app only capabilities bound to that slate and to you. The [chrome](/core-concepts/slate#the-chrome), served from a ContextLayer origin, shows who you are signed in as.
